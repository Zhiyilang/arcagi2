## Abstract

ARC-AGI-2's public evaluation set is adversarially filtered against program
synthesis: 109 of 120 tasks change the number of connected components, and the
median task edits more than 10% of its cells. We build a closed transform-DSL
solver anyway, because a closed library makes search exhaustive rather than
heuristic and therefore auditable: a program is emitted only when it
reproduces every demonstration pair exactly, so a wrong program can never be
submitted by accident. Measured on the 120 public tasks (167 test outputs), a
depth-2 enumeration of 469 candidates finds a consistent program for 1/120
tasks and predicts 0/167 test outputs correctly. We treat that as a result
rather than a failure and instrument it. A control battery of trivial families
— identity, both reflections, 180-degree rotation, global recolor — fits
0/120 tasks each, which falsifies the hypothesis that the low score is an
implementation defect. The battery also separates a real structural motif, the
demonstration outputs of 48/120 tasks are already well-formed periodic bands,
from the one family that fits anything, band periodicity repair at 1/120,
which then fails its own test 0/1. The contribution is a measured ceiling for
one approach on a benchmark built to defeat it, plus the control design that
converts a low score into a claim.

## Introduction

ARC entries are normally read in one dimension: score. A solver near zero is
then read only as a weak solver, and nothing further is learned. We argue a
second output is separable when the solver is built so that its search space
can be enumerated and its accept rule is exact. Such a solver can report
*coverage*: how much of a benchmark a given family could express at all,
under the same rule used to accept a submission. Coverage is not a score, and
it does not improve one, but it bounds what further engineering of that family
can buy.

That distinction matters here because ARC-AGI-2 was constructed to resist
synthesis. A coverage measurement on this set is therefore a measurement of
the resistance, and it is useful to the field in proportion to how
uncomfortable it is. Our number is uncomfortable: one task out of 120.

## Prior work

Two families dominate published ARC work. Program synthesis over a
hand-designed DSL appears throughout the 2024 and 2025 ARC Prize results
analyses at arcprize.org; direct LLM reasoning and test-time training grew to
dominate the leaderboard as model capability rose. Both optimize the same
objective, produce a correct output grid, and both are evaluated by score.
Neither, as far as we can find, is instrumented to report coverage — how many
tasks a given family could express even in principle, independent of whether
the search finds the program. The 2025 analysis notes that ARC-AGI-2 was
designed specifically to defeat synthesis approaches, and reports the
component-count and edit-distance statistics we cite; we are not aware of a
published measurement of the residual coverage of a closed family on the
public set. Our control battery supplies that number, and, more usefully,
supplies the design that makes such a number trustworthy.

## Approach

The solver is a closed library of grid transforms: seven modules, 469
enumerated candidates, each a frozen `(family, args)` record. Closed means the
enumeration is exhaustive, so consistency is a property of the search rather
than a hope about it. Parameters — recolor maps, pad targets, translations,
upscale factors — are inferred from the demonstrations and never guessed.
Search runs depth 1, then depth 2, then a depth-3 beam, deadline-bounded so a
run always terminates with a valid submission.

The accept rule is exact reproduction of every demonstration pair. This is
deliberately stricter than the scoring rule and it is the reason the artifact
is safe: official scoring credits either of two attempts, which rewards
precision over recall, so emitting a program that merely looks plausible is
strictly worse than emitting nothing. Attempt 1 is the simplest consistent
program; attempt 2 is the next-simplest with a *different family signature*,
so the two attempts fail independently rather than redundantly. With no
consistent program the solver falls back to identity plus a modal-color fill.

The measurement instrument is `ablations.py`. It applies the solver's own
accept rule to families chosen to be trivial, fits on the demonstrations,
accepts only on exact reproduction, then predicts, and reports two numbers
separately: how many tasks the family fits, and how many test outputs it then
gets right. Separating them is the whole point. A family that fits many tasks
and predicts none has told us something about the benchmark; a family that
fits many and predicts many has told us something about the benchmark too.
Conflating the two is how a solver talks itself into believing it works.
`ablations.py` also reports structural motif counts that are not fits at all,
which is how the band result below stays honest.

## Results

Measured on the official public evaluation set (120 tasks, 167 test outputs,
Apache-2.0, from `arcprize/ARC-AGI-2`), local either-attempt scoring, 10 s
per-task budget at depth 2:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Tasks with a consistent program (depth-2 DSL) | 1 / 120 |
| Test outputs correct (either attempt) | 0 / 167 |
| Offline assertions passing | 163 / 163 |

**Accuracy, stated plainly: 0 / 167.** We do not argue this is competitive.

The control battery, same accept rule, 120 tasks, about 8 seconds:

| Family | Tasks fitted | Test outputs correct |
|---|---|---|
| identity | 0 / 120 | — |
| flip horizontal | 0 / 120 | — |
| flip vertical | 0 / 120 | — |
| rotate 180 degrees | 0 / 120 | — |
| global recolor map | 0 / 120 | — |
| band periodicity repair | 1 / 120 (`135a2760`) | 0 / 1 |

Not one task in the public set is a pure recolor, reflection, rotation, or
copy. If our consistency check were broken, trivial families would fit tasks
in bulk; they fit none. This is the control that converts 1/120 from a
possible bug report into a measurement of the set.

The single consistent program, task `135a2760`, is a nested frame with
periodic content and an anomaly to repair. It reproduces every demonstration
pair and does not generalize to the test input — the case that motivates
reporting fits and predictions separately.

One motif survives the filter and is reported as a motif, not a fit: the
demonstration *outputs* of 48 / 120 tasks are already well-formed periodic
bands. Forty percent of the set has this structure, and it is not what
`band_repair` addresses — the demonstrations are already periodic, so there is
nothing to repair, and the input-to-output step is a *generation* of band
structure rather than a repair of it. That distinction is the most actionable
thing we found, and it is invisible to a score-only evaluation.

**Limits of the measurement.** The enumeration was run at depth 2 with a 10 s
per-task budget. Deeper search was not measured on the full set, so 1/120 is a
floor for this configuration, not a proof that these tasks are unreachable.
The 120 public tasks may differ from the private set.

## How this addresses the judging criteria

Accuracy is reported above and is low. Universality: the coverage instrument
transfers to any induction-from-demonstrations setting, and the
fit-versus-predict separation is domain-independent. Progress: it rules out a
region of the search space and prices it, which raises the expected value of
effort spent elsewhere on the 85% objective. Theory: the component-count and
edit-distance statistics explain *why* a closed family cannot reach these
tasks — the required operations create and destroy objects, which is open
composition, not enumeration. Completeness: every number here is regenerated
by `ablations.py` and pinned by 163 assertions; the notebook is self-contained
and MIT-licensed. Novelty: the trivial-family control, and reporting a motif
count that is deliberately not a fit, are not standard practice.

## Conclusion

We claim no score. We claim three things that survive a score of zero: a
ceiling for closed-DSL synthesis on ARC-AGI-2, measured rather than asserted
(1/120, with trivial-family controls at 0/120 that falsify the defect
hypothesis); a control design that any low-scoring entry can adopt to make its
low score informative; and a structural finding, 48/120 tasks whose
demonstration outputs are already periodic bands, indicating that the missing
operation is generation rather than repair. If the field's objective is 85%,
then knowing where a whole family cannot reach is worth more than another
entry that reports only that it did not.
